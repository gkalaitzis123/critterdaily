      About Ten Numbers

      How to play Ten Numbers, the daily estimation game

      Each day has ten trivia questions with a number for an answer, the same for everyone: how many people live somewhere, how tall a landmark is, how far apart two cities are, what year something happened. The closer your guess, the more points, up to 100 a question and 1,000 a day, and Nutmeg the squirrel gets an acorn for every 10.

      Do I need to know the exact answer?

      No, it's about estimating. How close counts as close depends on the question: a recent year has to be near the mark, a country's population only roughly right.

      Can I use miles and feet?

      Yes. Lengths and areas can be typed in metric or US units.

      Is it free?

      Yes, every day, with nothing to sign up for. Practice rounds are unlimited and don't count toward your stats.

      How to play

      • Ten questions a day, the same for everyone, each with a number for an answer, and at most five of them years: how many people live somewhere, how old a famous person was when they died, how tall a landmark is, how far apart two cities are, how fast or heavy something is, how many episodes a TV show ran, everyday counts like the keys on a piano, the year something happened or came out.
      • Guess as close as you can. Spot on earns 100 points, and you earn fewer the further off you are, but rarely none. Every 10 points is an acorn for Nutmeg's jar.
      • How close is close depends on the question. A film's running time needs to be within a few minutes, and a population or a number of species within about half or double. A year needs to be within a few years if it's recent, and gets more room the longer ago it was, up to 80 years for ancient history. Astronomical numbers get more room too: for the ants on Earth, ten times off still earns 76 points.
      • After each guess, a number line shows the answer, where your guess landed, and the green band that earns 70 points or more.
      • Type numbers however you like: 15,000, 15k or 2.5 million. On a phone, type 2.5 and pick million under the box, or pick BC for a year before Christ. Under the box, the game reads back what it understood.
      • Lengths and areas can be answered in meters or feet, kilometers or miles: pick under the box.
      • A day is out of 1,000 points. Nut Nibbler, Acorn Gatherer, Stash Keeper, Hoard Master or Squirrel Sage?
      • Want more? Practice rounds use random questions, as many as you like, and don't count toward your stats.
