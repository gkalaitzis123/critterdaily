One word, two clues: how Crisscross is written

November 16, 2026, updated November 20 · 3 minute read · about Crisscross

Crisscross is a small crossword: a five-by-five grid, ten words, usually one to three minutes. Small doesn't mean simple to make. Here's how the puzzles are put together, and why the same word can feel different the second time you meet it.

Filling the grid

A five-by-five grid has 25 squares. Crisscross usually blacks out a few in the corners, which leaves ten words, five across and five down, with nearly every white square in two of them. That's the hard part: every letter has to work in two words at once. The words come from ENABLE, a public-domain word list, chosen with wordfreq, which measures how common each word is, so the grid is filled with words you know rather than the odd abbreviations crosswords sometimes lean on.

Two clues for every word

Every word in Crisscross has two clues written for it, a plain one and a playful one. Over this year's puzzles, 1,324 different words appear, and many come back. When one does, it usually comes with its other clue, so a word you've seen doesn't feel like a repeat. A few pairs from this year:

  • CLAY: "Potter's material" and "Surface at the French Open"
  • EAGLE: "Bald ___ (U.S. symbol)" and "Two under par, on the links"
  • DELAY: "Put off" and "Dreaded word on a flight board"
  • DEER: "Bambi, for one" and "Like a ___ in the headlights"
  • BOARD: "Get on, as a plane" and "Bored? Try a ___ game"
  • ANGER: "Fury" and "Red emotion in Pixar's Inside Out"

Some words come back more than others. AREA appears 15 times over the year, ONES 14, and YES and PER 13 each: short words made of common letters fit almost any corner of a grid.

Reading a clue

Crossword clues follow a few habits that are worth knowing:

  • The clue matches the answer's form. "Finished" is ENDED, not END, and a plural clue wants a plural answer.
  • "E.g." or "say" means an example. "Square footage, e.g." is AREA: square footage is one kind of area.
  • A blank is a fill-in. "All you ___ is love (Beatles)" is NEED.
  • A comma adds a setting. "Two under par, on the links" means in golf, so it's EAGLE.
  • A question mark means play. "Bored? Try a ___ game" sounds like one word and means another.

Solving #3, square by square

October 29's grid was the quickest of the first month: the typical solver needed 29 seconds. Here's an order that gets there.

  • Start with the surest clue. 1 Across, "Not good", is three letters: BAD. That gives the first letters of 1, 2 and 3 Down.
  • Use them at once. 1 Down, "Bored? Try a ___ game", starts with B: BOARD. 3 Down, "Put off", starts with D: DELAY.
  • Now the across words are half done. 4 Across, "It doesn't grow on trees, they say", reads _O_E_: MONEY. 6 Across, "Bald ___ (U.S. symbol)", reads _A_L_: EAGLE. 7 Across, "Trick-or-___", reads _R_A_: TREAT.
  • The rest fills itself. The middle column now reads A, N, G, E and R, the red emotion in Inside Out. 4 Down is MET, 5 Down is YET, and 8 Across, "Like the Sahara", is DRY.

Every word in that grid is everyday English, which is why it went so fast: once two down words are in, each across word is half filled before you read its clue.

Racing the clock

Crisscross times you from Start to the last right letter, and your time earns a title, from Hatchling to Silk Sprinter, for 45 seconds or less. The fastest solvers don't read every clue first: they start with the surest one, then use each new letter as a free hint for the word crossing it. Check and Reveal are there when you're stuck, at 10 seconds each. In the first month, half of all solves took under 52 seconds, so a minute is a fair first target.
