The least-read answers in Deep Cut

November 16, 2026, updated November 20 · 3 minute read · about Deep Cut

Deep Cut has 254 lists and more than 58,000 answers, each with a Wikipedia article and a count of how many people read it. Sort any list by its readers and the top holds few surprises. The bottom is where it gets interesting. Here's what it looks like on eleven of them, with each answer's share of everyone reading about that list.

Roman emperors

Augustus (1 in 14), Marcus Aurelius (1 in 15) and Nero (1 in 20) lead. At the bottom are short-lived usurpers: Sabinian and Marcellus, at about 1 in 21,000, and Septimius, at about 1 in 55,000, read about once a day.

US states

California (1 in 20), Hawaii (1 in 21) and Texas (1 in 28) top the list. Idaho (1 in 78), South Dakota (1 in 105) and North Dakota (1 in 107) are the least read, though North Dakota's article still gets more than 1,200 readers a day. The spread is small, but Deep Cut measures rarity within each list, so North Dakota digs as deep as any forgotten emperor.

Countries

The United States alone gets almost 50,000 readers a day, about 1 in 19 of everyone reading about countries; the United Kingdom (1 in 58) and India (1 in 66) follow. The least read are Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (1 in 690), Antigua and Barbuda (1 in 742) and the Federated States of Micronesia (1 in 778).

Bones of the human body

The clavicle (1 in 19), sternum (1 in 20) and coccyx (1 in 23) are the famous ones. The deep cuts are in your hand: the trapezoid bone of the wrist (1 in 836), and the second and third metacarpals (1 in 1,175 and 1 in 1,563).

Dinosaurs

Of 700 dinosaurs, Tyrannosaurus is 1 in 18, Allosaurus 1 in 37 and Spinosaurus 1 in 41. Nanyangosaurus, Aepisaurus and Xixianykus are about 1 in 8,500 to 8,900, with five or six readers a day.

Chemical elements

Gold (1 in 34), aluminium (1 in 42) and mercury (1 in 43) lead. The least read are heavy synthetic elements, made a few atoms at a time in laboratories: mendelevium (1 in 537), dubnium (1 in 621) and bohrium (1 in 630).

Classical composers

Mozart leads at 1 in 17, and John Williams, the film composer, comes second at 1 in 25, just ahead of Beethoven (1 in 26). At the bottom of 500 are Juan Crisóstomo de Arriaga, Ferdinando Carulli and Karl Goldmark, each about 1 in 4,200.

Dog breeds

The Dachshund (1 in 44), Cane Corso (1 in 51) and Golden Retriever (1 in 64) lead 628 breeds. The least read are crossbreeds with names to match: the Poochon, the Beaglier and the Gerberian Shepsky, each read about once a day, around 1 in 100,000.

Rivers of Europe

The Danube (1 in 10), Rhine (1 in 18) and Thames (1 in 21) lead 301 rivers. The Iller, Guadalete and Vyatka, each about 1 in 2,500 to 3,000, are the least read.

European capitals

London leads (1 in 14), and Monaco (1 in 18) is read more than Paris (1 in 19). The least read are Vaduz (1 in 245), Andorra la Vella (1 in 287) and San Marino (1 in 541).

Dutch and Flemish painters

Vincent van Gogh gets 1 in every 4 readers of the list's 277 painters, by far the biggest share on any list here; Hieronymus Bosch (1 in 12) and Rembrandt (1 in 13) follow. The least read, Lucas Achtschellinck, Frans Ykens and Bernard Accama, are about 1 in 13,000 to 14,500.

What the bottom of a list tells you

The least-read answers follow a few patterns: small places, short reigns, parts within parts, recent discoveries and recent crossbreeds. The top, meanwhile, is often one name read far more than the rest. That's what makes Deep Cut work: almost everyone knows something deeper than the first answer that comes to mind, if they stop to look for it.
