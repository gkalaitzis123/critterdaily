How to read Borderline's clues: the first day, played map by map

November 20, 2026 · 3 minute read · about Borderline

In Borderline, the wrong guesses are where the game happens. Each one tells you how far away the mystery is, which way to go, and whether your guess existed in the year shown. Here is Borderline's first day, #1 on October 30, played map by map, with the clues the game gives for each guess.

Map 1: a whole sea, in the year 150

The first map each day is the easiest of the three. This one is shaded all around the Mediterranean, from Spain to Egypt and from Britain to the Black Sea, in the year 150.

Few states have ever held a whole sea, and in 150 there's one obvious candidate: the Roman Empire. It's right the first time, for the full 6 points.

Suppose you'd guessed the Byzantine Empire instead, the eastern half of Rome that outlived the west. The game would have said the mystery was about 1,400 km northwest, and that the Byzantine Empire began 245 years after 150. The second part is the useful one. "Began after" means the answer came before your guess, so think of what held this land earlier.

Map 2: the right place, the wrong time

The second map shades the southern two-thirds of the Korean peninsula in the year 1000.

The natural first guess is South Korea. The game answers that the mystery is nearby, so the place is right, but that South Korea began 948 years after 1000, so the time is wrong. "Nearby" at the wrong time means you need whoever ruled this land then. In the year 1000 that was Goryeo, the kingdom that ruled Korea from 918 to 1392 and gave the country its name in English. It's found on the second guess, for 5 points.

Map 3: a country that wasn't there yet

The last map is the hardest. It shades a band of land in Central America in 1825, between Mexico and Panama. A good first guess is Guatemala, the region's most populous country today. The game says the mystery is about 420 km southeast, and that Guatemala began 15 years after 1825. So try Nicaragua, further along the isthmus. Now the mystery is about 240 km northwest, and Nicaragua, too, began 15 years after 1825.

Two guesses, and the mystery sits between them: southeast of Guatemala and northwest of Nicaragua, in a year before either existed. After two wrong guesses comes the first hint: today, this land is in Nicaragua, Honduras, Guatemala and Costa Rica. A state that held the land of four of today's countries before any of them existed must have been a union of them, and in 1825 there was one: the United Provinces of Central America, also called the Federal Republic of Central America, which broke apart between 1838 and 1841. It's found on the third guess, for 4 points.

The day's score

That's 6, 5 and 4 points: 15 of a possible 18, with all three found in six guesses in all, which makes you a Master Cartographer. In the first month, the average day's score was 9.

What the day teaches

  • Read the year before the shape. In 150, a shape around a whole sea can only be one empire. In 1825, Spain's former colonies were forming new states and unions, and some of them didn't last.
  • "Began after" means go back. The answer is whatever held the land before your guess existed.
  • "Nearby" at the wrong time means the right land. Ask who ruled it in that year.
  • Box it in with two guesses. A miss on each side tells you the mystery lies between them.
  • Take the hints. If the land is spread over several of today's countries, think of an empire or a union that held them all.
