The languages Mother Tongue players find hardest

November 18, 2026, updated November 20 · 4 minute read · about Mother Tongue

Every finished game of Mother Tongue sends one anonymous number: how many guesses it took to hatch the egg, or that it didn't hatch. Put together, a month of those numbers shows which languages are easy to find, which are hard, and why.

Where the numbers come from

This note covers Mother Tongue #3 to #32, October 19 to November 17, leaving out the one day that fewer than 20 people played. That's 4,291 finished games. Across them, players found the language 80% of the time, in 8.5 guesses on average. Each connection counts once a day, and nothing ties a score to a person; the privacy page explains how.

The easiest: a country's main language

The languages nearly everyone found were the main languages of big countries:

  • French (#24, November 9): found by 95% of players, in 5.1 guesses on average. Twelve players got it with their very first guess.
  • Mandarin Chinese (#17, November 2): 95%, in 5.2 guesses.
  • Italian (#12, October 28): 91%, in 5.4.
  • Finnish (#27, November 12): 88%, in 6.4.
  • Polish (#32, November 17): 93%, in 6.8.

Days like these go quickly because every clue agrees. The compass points at a country, the family tree confirms the branch, and the language you'd name for that country is the right one.

The hardest: a country's other languages

Most of the hardest days were the opposite: languages spoken inside a country that's best known for a different one, like these.

  • Asturian (#28, November 13), from northern Spain: found by 60%, in 11.2 guesses.
  • Kannada (#31, November 16), from southern India: 64%, in 12.3.
  • Occitan (#22, November 7), from southern France: 66%, in 11.2.
  • Breton (#30, November 15), from Brittany in France: 67%, in 11.1.
  • Scots (#26, November 11), from Scotland: 74%, in 9.5.

Here the clues pull in different directions. The compass points straight at Spain or France, so the natural guess is Spanish or French, and the family tree only takes you so far from there. Spanish shares the West Iberian branch with Asturian: close, but not a match. French shares only Western Romance with Occitan, and nothing below Indo-European with Breton, which is a Celtic language, closer to Welsh. Hindi isn't related to Kannada at all: Kannada is Dravidian, like Tamil, and guessing Tamil puts you on the South Dravidian branch. English and Scots share every named branch down to Anglic, so the tree can't take you any further; you have to think of Scots.

Fridays dig deeper

Six days a week the mystery is one of the world's 150 best-known languages. On Fridays it comes from the next 25, and it shows. The four Fridays so far brought Chamorro, from Guam, on October 23, when only 4 of the 17 players found it; Mauritian Creole on October 30 (72%); Ilocano, from the northern Philippines, on November 6; and Asturian on November 13.

Ilocano was the hardest of all the days that at least 20 people played: only 51% found it, in 13.8 guesses on average. The compass points at the Philippines, and Tagalog, the language most people know from there, shares only the Malayo-Polynesian branch with Ilocano, a branch of more than a thousand languages.

The hints arrive when they're needed

Mother Tongue gives a hint after 5 guesses (how many people speak the language), after 10 (the countries where it's spoken) and after 15 (its first letter). On the five hardest days, the typical winner needed 10 to 14 guesses, so the country hint often did the work: the median for Ilocano was 14 guesses and for Kannada 13. On the easiest days the median was 4 to 6, before most hints.

Tips from a month of eggs

  • When the compass points into a big country, think beyond its main language. Spain also has Catalan, Galician, Basque and Asturian; France has Occitan, Breton and Corsican; Britain has Welsh, Scottish Gaelic and Scots; the Philippines has Cebuano and Ilocano as well as Tagalog.
  • Check the family before the country. If a guess of Hindi comes back unrelated, try a Dravidian language like Tamil or Telugu.
  • Type a branch's name, like Celtic or Dravidian, to see the languages in it, then pick the one spoken nearest the compass.
  • On Fridays, expect a smaller language. Regional languages and creoles are fair game.
