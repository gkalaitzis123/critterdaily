Critter Daily's first month, in numbers

November 18, 2026 · 3 minute read · about all six games

Critter Daily's first three games went live on October 17, and the other three followed over the next two weeks. By November 17, players had finished 25,534 daily games. Each one sends a single anonymous score, so together they show how each game is played and which days were the hardest.

From dozens a day to hundreds

In each game's first week, an average day saw 41 finished games of Deep Cut, 29 of Ten Numbers and 22 of Mother Tongue. In the week to November 17, those were 400, 304 and 247. Burrow went from 31 a day to 233, Crisscross from 91 to 226, and Borderline, the newest game, from 63 to 148. Deep Cut's busiest day was the last one counted, November 17, with 555 finished games.

Deep Cut: 124 meters down

Since Deep Cut moved to eight prompts a day on October 28, the average dig has reached 124 meters, a meter short of the Permian. The deepest day was November 17 (#32), at 151 meters on average, into the Cambrian; the shallowest was October 29 (#13), at 70. The three deepest days were all in November, so players may be getting the hang of it. What 58,000 Deep Cut answers reveal looks at the answers themselves.

Mother Tongue: found four times in five

Players found the mystery language in 80% of games, in 8.5 guesses on average. French was the easiest (95% found it, in 5.1 guesses) and Ilocano, from the northern Philippines, the hardest (51%, in 13.8). The languages Mother Tongue players find hardest explains why.

Ten Numbers: about 500 points a day

Since Ten Numbers moved to scores out of 1,000 with its #5, the average score has been 498. The hardest was October 24 (#8), at 413 points: a day of numbers few people carry around, like how many people died in the 1906 San Francisco earthquake (about 3,000) and how many live in Skopje (about 422,500). The friendliest was November 12 (#27), at 584: a day of dates and figures many people know, like the year the American Civil War began (1861) and how many people are alive today (about 8.3 billion).

Burrow: 67 meters down

Clover the rabbit dug 67 meters in an average game. The deepest day was November 15 (#29), at 87 meters, and the shallowest October 26 (#9), at 38.

Crisscross: under a minute

Half of all Crisscross solves took less than 52 seconds, and 56% took under a minute. The quickest day was October 29 (#3), when the typical solver needed 29 seconds for a grid of everyday words like MONEY, EAGLE and TREAT. The slowest was November 6 (#11), at 80 seconds, with MEDIA, CROP, SONS and MERE.

Borderline: half the points

Borderline scores each day out of 18: up to 6 points for each of the three maps, one fewer for each extra guess. The average score was 9. The toughest was November 9 (#11), with Cameroon today, the Kushan Empire in the year 150 and Mitanni in 1500 BCE: it averaged 5 points, and 22% of players found none of the three. The friendliest scored 11 on average: November 5 (#7), with Cambodia today, Serbia and Montenegro in 2005 and the Socialist Republic of Romania in 1975, and November 16 (#18), with the Russian Empire in 1890, the Kalmar Union in 1430 and Denmark–Norway in 1550.

How these numbers are kept

Every finished daily game sends its score to Critter Daily's own small database, kept with a code scrambled from the connection, so each connection counts once a day and nothing ties a score to a person. Practice rounds aren't sent. The privacy page has the details. We'll keep counting, and write up what the numbers show.
