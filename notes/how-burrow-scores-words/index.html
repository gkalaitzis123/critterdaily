Rare words, deep burrows: how Burrow scores words

November 16, 2026 · 2 minute read · about Burrow

Burrow gives you seven letters and a minute. Every word you make digs Clover the rabbit's burrow deeper, but words aren't all worth the same: rare words dig deeper than common ones. Here's exactly how.

Measuring how rare a word is

Burrow's words come from ENABLE, a public-domain word list many word games use, and are kept only if they turn up in real English. How often each one turns up comes from wordfreq, a research project that counts words across books, news, film subtitles, websites and more. It gives each word a Zipf frequency: about 7 for the most common words in English and around 1 or 2 for very rare ones. "Note" scores a little over 5; "tenon", a joint in woodworking, a little over 2.

From rarity to meters

A word digs one meter for every third of a point it falls below 6.5, so from 1 meter for the commonest words to 14 for the rarest. Each letter past four adds 2 meters, and a word that uses all seven letters adds 10 more. So "note" digs 4 meters and "tenon" 15, and a rare seven-letter word reaches 30, the deepest a single word can go.

An example day

On Burrow's first day, the letters were P, P, U, S, O, R and E, and there were 56 words to find. "Sure", one of the most common words in English, dug 2 meters, and "rose" and "user" 5 each. The seven-letter word was "purpose", worth 21 meters on its own: a common word, but long, and using every letter. "Poseur" dug 18, and "sprue", a channel in a casting mold, 15.

The deepest words

Across the year's 365 days there are 19,646 words to find, about 54 a day, and every day has at least one seven-letter word; some have four. The deepest words of all are rare seven-letter ones, which reach the 30-meter limit: "scleral", "abridge", "treadle", "scalene" and "maunder" among them. On a good day, one of those is worth as much as several common words put together.

How to dig deeper

  • Find the seven-letter word. It's worth at least 17 meters, 10 for using every letter, 6 for its length and at least 1 for its rarity, and every day has one.
  • Prefer odd words to many words. A minute isn't long, and a rare five-letter word like "tenon", at 15 meters, is worth three or four common ones.
  • Think of trades and hobbies. Words from woodworking, sewing, sailing or science are often on the list and rarely in everyday speech, and that's exactly what digs.
  • Shuffle when you stall. A new order shows new pairs of letters, and new words with them.

What doesn't count

Names and abbreviations aren't in the word list, and rude and offensive words are taken out, using a public list of them and a few more found by hand. If you ever meet a word in Burrow that shouldn't be there, please write to rjenkins1445@gmail.com.
