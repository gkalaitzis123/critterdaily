          How to play Deep Cut

          Deep Cut is a free daily trivia game from Critter Daily with a twist: every right answer counts, but the less famous your answer, the more it's worth. Each day has eight prompts, the same for everyone, and Digby the mole digs deeper with every obscure answer you find.

          The rules

          • Each prompt asks you to name one thing from a list, like "Name a Roman emperor", "Name a cheese" or "Name a bone in the human body".
          • You have 30 seconds a prompt. Type any answer that fits. Wrong guesses cost nothing but time, and your first right answer is locked in.
          • Spelling needn't be perfect. Small typos count, and if you're close, Digby asks "Did you mean…?" Accents, punctuation and a leading "the" don't matter, and common other names count too.

          How an answer is scored

          Deep Cut measures how obscure an answer is with Wikipedia. For each prompt it adds up how many people read the article of every answer on the list over the last 60 days, then works out your answer's share. Augustus is read by about 1 in 14 of everyone reading about Roman emperors; Trebonianus Gallus by about 1 in 290.

          The most-read answer on a list digs 1 meter and the rarest digs 50, with everything between spaced by how many times rarer it is, so every prompt can go just as deep. Your meters add up over the eight prompts, and Digby digs back through Earth's history: the Holocene, the Pleistocene (60 m), the Cretaceous (80 m), the Jurassic (100 m), the Permian (125 m) and, deepest of all, the Cambrian (150 m).

          Tips for digging deep

          • Skip the first answer that comes to mind. Everyone else thinks of it too, so it's usually the most-read one on the list. Go for the second or third thing you know.
          • Think small and specific: a regional cheese instead of cheddar, a short-lived emperor instead of Augustus, a little bone of the wrist instead of the femur.
          • Things in the news are read a lot. Older and quieter answers dig deeper.
          • Keep a safe answer in reserve. If your deep cut isn't on the list, you still have time for a sure one, and any right answer digs at least a meter.

          Where the lists come from

          Each prompt's list is built from Wikidata, the free database behind Wikipedia, and every answer on it has its own English Wikipedia article. How rare an answer is comes from Wikipedia's public page-view counts. Both are public domain, so after you play you can look up the answers you didn't know.

          Questions

          Is it free? Yes, every day, with nothing to sign up for. Practice rounds use other prompts, as many as you like, and don't count toward your stats.

          When is there a new one? Every midnight, your time. Everyone gets the same prompts on the same date, so you can compare with friends.

          Where are my stats kept? In your own browser, on your device. There's no account: your streak and history stay with you, and clearing the site's data erases them.
